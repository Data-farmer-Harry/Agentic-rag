Evaluating Planning and Execution in Agent Trajectories
Babak Barazandeh1∗, Subhabrata Majumdar2, George Michailidis3

Abstract
Large language model agents solve tasks by generating tra-
jectories that interleave planning, tool calls, and intermediate
results. Current evaluation metrics reduce such a trajectory
to a binary success flag or compare it against a reference by
exact matching. A success flag cannot distinguish a sound
solution from one that succeeds by luck, and says nothing
about why a failed run went wrong. Exact matching penal-
izes plans that are valid but reordered or decomposed dif-
ferently from the reference. We reframe trajectory evaluation
as a distance between the agent’s execution graph and a set
of valid solution graphs, and instantiate it via an unbalanced
fused Gromov-Wasserstein transport problem over attributed
dependency graphs. The resulting score, termedOtap(Opti-
mal Transport for Agentic Planning), is a pseudo-metric that
is provably invariant to dependency-preserving reorderings
and has bounded sensitivity to redundant steps. Its unbal-
ancedmarginalshandlemissingorhallucinatedstepswithout
forcing a match, and its soft coupling accommodates varia-
tioninplangranularity.Oncontrolledperturbationsandthree
public benchmarks,Otapseparates valid from invalid trajec-
tories in a regime where semantics-only metrics score below
chance. Its accuracy is highest when the dependency graph is
recovered exactly, and drops only when the graph is inferred
heuristically from free-text traces.

OnCPU,oneOtapevaluationofasix-steptrajectoryagainst
K=3references—including all Sinkhorn and conditional-
gradient iterations, excluding encoder cache warm-up—
takes28–33ms across the controlled, PlanBench, and We-

training loop as a process reward.
6.6 Human evaluation
Validatingametricrequirestrajectorieswhoserelativequal-
ity is known. Our evidence therefore comes in two tiers.
Thefirstisafullyautomatic,reproducibleprotocolinwhich
ground-truth quality orderings holdby construction: a cor-
pus of plan DAGs is subjected to controlled perturbations
of known severity, and a metric is measured by how often
it recovers the resulting ordering (Part A) and by its corre-
lation with a graded corruption ladder (Part B). The second
tier moves to three public benchmarks: PlanBench, where
720real LLM-generated planscarry oracle validity labels
at zero annotation cost; and GAIA and WebArena, where
the perturbation protocol is re-run on real human-authored
solutions and real task intents, respectively. Across all stud-
ies (3,092trajectories in total) we use a single fixed seed
and thea prioridefault hyper-parameters of Sec. 4; no tun-
ing of any kind was performed on any corpus. We release
the full harness—corpora, loaders, perturbation generator,
all metric implementations, and seeds—for one-command
reproduction.
